How this simulation works

The outcome is change in eGFR from baseline to 12 months, in mL/min/1.73 m² (shown as “units” throughout). eGFR estimates how well the kidneys filter blood. Negative changes indicate a decline; a more positive change means better preservation of kidney function. The two groups are simulated from normal distributions with a shared SD, a usual-care mean change of −4 units, and a treatment mean change of −4 + δ units. Thus, a positive δ can represent slower decline, even if both groups lose kidney function. The treatment, values, prior beliefs and benefit threshold are hypothetical teaching choices. The normal sampling model is a teaching simplification.

The chosen effect prior is independent of the variance prior: p(δ, σ²) = p(δ) × p(σ²), with σ² ~ Inv-Gamma(ν₀/2, ν₀σ₀²/2), using the shape/scale parameterisation. The control-group mean has a flat prior. Integrating out the control mean and σ² gives L(δ) ∝ [ν₀σ₀² + SSwithin + (n/2)(δ − d)²]^−(ν₀ + 2n − 1)/2, where d is the observed difference and n is patients per arm.

The posterior is calculated by numerical integration of this likelihood times the selected effect prior. The likelihood curve is normalised over δ for display and uses the variance prior when integrating out σ². The posterior is generally not a member of the selected prior family. The data-only comparison instead reports the usual pooled two-sample t confidence interval; it has no posterior probability. All Bayesian intervals are equal-tailed 95% credible intervals.

Normal scale is its SD; Student-t and Cauchy scale are scale parameters; Laplace scale is b (SD = √2 b); uniform scale is its half-width. Student-t has a finite SD only above 2 degrees of freedom; the Cauchy has neither a finite mean nor a finite variance. Compare families thoughtfully: matching their scales does not match their prior information.
