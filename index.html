Does a new therapy clear BK viraemia after kidney transplant?

Kidney recipients with BK viraemia receive standard care (reduced immunosuppression), with or without a hypothetical new therapy. This is the example from the explainer video Bayesian trials, explained with BK virus.

InterpretationPositive favours the treatment
The clinical motivation: BK virus, few patients, and the prior

BK polyomavirus viraemia is common in the first year after kidney transplantation. It can progress to BK nephropathy and graft loss, and there is no proven antiviral therapy, so reducing immunosuppression remains standard care. Trials are small, which is where a Bayesian design helps: it combines earlier evidence with the accumulating data and answers the question clinicians ask: given the data, how likely is it that the therapy works?

The Video prior (sceptical) gives each arm a Beta(8, 12) prior: about 40% clearance on standard care, worth about 20 patients, and deliberately sceptical, with no benefit assumed. Compare it with other priors on the treatment effect, then move the benefit threshold. A gain of 10 percentage points is the illustrative "meaningful" threshold used in the video.

The video's pre-specified rules look at 20 and 40 patients per arm: stop for success if P(δ > 0) > 97.5%, and for futility if P(δ > 10 points) < 10%. The trial and every number are hypothetical, for teaching.
